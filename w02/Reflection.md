# Week 02 — Reflection

## 1. What topic helped you understand machine learning better, and why?

Understanding training and inference helped me build a clearer picture of ML. I learned that training allows a model to identify patterns from existing data, while inference uses those learned patterns to produce results for new inputs.

## 2. Which ML concepts did you initially mix up, and how do you tell them apart now?

I found classification and regression confusing at first. Classification predicts categories, such as Pass or Fail, whereas regression estimates numerical values, such as chip power consumption or circuit delay.

## 3. What did you verify, and what did you learn from the evidence?

I reviewed Google's ML learning resources to understand how models are evaluated. I learned that high accuracy alone does not guarantee a model is useful, especially when it fails to identify important cases such as defective products.

## 4. What can make an ML model unreliable after deployment?

A model may become less reliable when real-world conditions change or when new data differs from its training examples. Regular monitoring and evaluation can help identify these problems before they seriously affect decisions.

## 5. What would you like to explore next in machine learning?

I want to understand how algorithms adjust their parameters during training and how engineers choose suitable features and evaluation metrics for practical problems, particularly in semiconductor physical design.
